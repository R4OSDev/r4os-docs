R4OS memory management
======================

Kernel memory
-------------

The physical-frame allocator owns usable frames after Limine handoff. Page
tables, kernel mappings, MMIO and the higher-half direct map remain kernel
responsibilities. Frame zero is never returned by normal allocation.

Kernel heap and eager VM extents
--------------------------------

The kernel heap commits at least 16 pages when it must grow and doubles its
next step up to 256 pages. A request larger than that bounded step still
commits its exact page requirement. If a speculative extension cannot be
satisfied, the heap retries only the exact requirement. This replaces the
former exact grow after almost every medium allocation.

A free heap tail is retained between allocations. Its low watermark is at
least 16 pages and is bounded by roughly 1/1024 of the heap's RAM-derived cap,
up to 256 pages. Normal release starts only above twice that watermark. When
free PMM frames fall to 1/32 of installed frames or 512 frames, whichever is
larger, retention becomes zero and the heap returns every releasable trailing
page except its one-page structural floor. Full 0xDD payload poisoning remains
mandatory. Commit, uncommit, suppressed-release, pressure-release and poison
byte counters make the time/memory tradeoff observable.

Eager non-pageable VM commits acquire naturally adjacent PMM frames in
batches of at most 64 pages. They never invoke the allocator's whole-memory
contiguous search. Each natural extent uses one physical-block claim and one
bounded mapping transaction; a mapping failure removes every PTE installed by
that transaction before returning. The complete virtual extent is zeroed
before publication. Uncommit first verifies the exact contiguous PTE/frame
run, then removes it and releases its physical-block ownership as one prepared
transaction. Demand-paged R4X faults and Page-State transitions remain
page-granular.

The short SMP4 acceptance profile performs 16 sequential 192-KiB heap churn
iterations and publishes `HEAPPROBE`. It requires bounded commit/uncommit
calls, restored live-byte/block counts, bounded retained pages and the full
poison-byte delta; it is not a subsystem long-run test.

Program memory
--------------

Each program owns a stable heap-backed instance object and its payloads.
Virtual regions reserve address ranges independently of physical commitment.
Pages move through explicit states; page-in, writeback, eviction and pagefile
ownership are recorded rather than inferred from addresses or values.
Allocation failure and incomplete enumeration are visible errors.

`vm_commit` flags 0 retain lazy R4X VM commitment. The additive
`vm_commit_flag_resident` prepares zeroed physical RAM and pins it before
returning success. Each call covers at most 256 KiB (64 pages), uses the
no-sleep VM owner with ordered page-table/physical owners and performs no
filesystem/pagefile/reclaim wait.
Profile commit/resident limits and the kernel's physical reserve apply.
Failure can retain a partially committed region when safe rollback cannot
finish; the caller must release it and must not use failed output. Pins last
until decommit/release. Physical statistics alone do not reserve memory.
Recovery uses this operation before any destructive step and retains the
original ZIP, decoded images, imported files and prepared NTFS metadata.
Sparse decommit combines only verified contiguous PTE/frame runs of at most
64 pages; timeout keeps the existing partial-uncommit retry state and frames.

The Zig SDK allocator behind `ctx.allocator()` keeps small allocations in
64-MiB reserved regions that are committed in bounded increments. Twenty
geometric free-size classes cover the measured block layout from 128 bytes to
the full region; allocation therefore visits free candidates instead of every
occupied block. Blocks at or above 1 MiB, and alignments above one page, retain
their direct VM-region path.

Every SDK block carries a checked header/footer boundary tag. Free-list
removal, predecessor lookup, coalescing and region-end lookup are constant-time
metadata operations; split, merge and top decommit update the size-class index
as one transaction. Invalid headers, footers or list links stop the operation
and increment the allocator corruption counter. Failed decommit and owner-bound release calls may already have removed
pages. Their exact retry identity stays outside the affected memory; those
pages cannot return to allocation indices before teardown is confirmed.
One allocator-local atomic lock serializes these metadata and VM mutations
between threads of the same R4X instance; contended program threads yield
through R4SYS, while payload access and remap copying remain outside the lock.
`allocatorStats()` exposes candidate and class searches, boundary probes,
splits, coalesces, corruptions and VM-call counts alongside the existing byte
and allocation totals. The additional per-block footer/link metadata is the
intentional space tradeoff for removing the former linear paths.

Small regions initially commit 64 KiB. Successful extensions grow
geometrically up to a 1-MiB step, while a single larger request may still ask
for exactly its bounded need. Normal free keeps at least 256 KiB per used
region and decommits a free tail only when at least 512 KiB can be returned;
the 32-region ceiling therefore bounds this ordinary retained floor to 8 MiB
per process. Explicit or allocation-failure reclaim reduces free tails to the
single metadata page and resets their growth step.

Direct allocations of at least 1 MiB can retain one exact-sized region in
each of three bounded buckets up to 2, 4 and 8 MiB. Regions caused only by a
large alignment, regions above 8 MiB and a second free region in an occupied
bucket are released directly. The combined cache is capped at 14 MiB and
never changes caller alignment or zero-size behavior. A reuse hit needs no
reserve, commit, decommit or release operation. `allocatorTrim()` releases
all cached direct regions and the reclaimable small-region tails; VM
allocation failures perform that same bounded cleanup before at most one
retry. Failed releases remain represented and retryable, while kernel owner
cleanup remains the final process-exit boundary.

Allocator statistics additionally expose cached entries/bytes, cache hits,
misses and evictions, current and peak commit, requested decommit bytes, trim
count and successfully reclaimed bytes. These counters distinguish reduced
VM churn from the deliberately bounded retained-memory tradeoff.

The system is resource-limited, not compiled around fixed program, task or
thread capacities. Chunked registries may grow and shrink while keeping live
objects and embedded synchronization primitives at stable addresses.

Stack memory
------------

Program-stack address reservation and physical commitment are separate.
Normal, service and desktop profiles reserve 4 MiB; large-service and
build-tool reserve 8 MiB; tiny reserves 2 MiB. Browser and workstation retain
32 MiB pending a representative workload. Initial commits are 64 KiB for
tiny, normal and service, 128 KiB for desktop, large-service and build-tool,
and 256 KiB for browser/workstation. A moving guard precedes the committed
span, which grows in 64-KiB steps. Canary high-water and create/release-cycle
telemetry justify these profile values without turning observed counts into
hard task capacities.

Each scheduler Task separately owns a 64-KiB committed kernel stack and a
16-KiB uncommitted guard reservation. A measured 39,800-byte maximum and the
historical real overflow at 32 KiB prohibit reducing that committed size.
At most eight released kernel stacks remain reclaimably cached and four
critical stacks remain pre-reserved for progress under pressure.

Indexed metadata
----------------

Physical block ownership is indexed by an AVL interval tree, a block-ID hash
and a free-slot bitmap. A successful 4-KiB claim therefore searches the
physical neighborhood and available metadata words instead of repeatedly
walking all 8192 block records. Split and merge updates are journaled; a late
error restores the touched records and their indices before returning.

Virtual ranges maintain a sorted address index in addition to their stable ID
index. Address faults use binary lookup. Commit spans and Page-State spans are
linked to their owning range and use global constant-time free lists, so an
operation visits only that range's ordered metadata. Boundary slots needed by
partial updates are reserved before mutation, adjacent compatible spans are
coalesced locally, and release removes mappings and physical ownership before
discarding the range metadata.

VM reclaim keeps a range-and-page cursor between returned frames and between
calls. Multi-frame reclaim resumes after the last inspected page rather than
starting at the first virtual range for every frame. Range, span and page
steps, index probes, rollbacks and rebuilds are exported through the R4DEV
performance snapshot for conformance and tail analysis.

Page-cache payload pressure
---------------------------

The filesystem page cache keeps 512 fixed resident metadata slots but admits
only 64 through 512 payload frames (256 KB through 2048 KB/2 MB). Its boot-RAM
limit and current free-frame pressure independently bound the active capacity.
Contraction first reclaims clean payloads and routes dirty entries through
normal writeback. Speculative read-ahead is disabled for 0.75.6 after its
interaction with multi-page demand fills reproducibly stalled the subsystem
workload; R4DEV exports a zero window and zero activity. Capacity limits,
pressure state and trims remain observable in the performance snapshot.

Contiguous cache fills use resident kernel heap staging only while their exact
PMM payload identities are `io_busy`-pinned. Program-facing reads then copy via
resident per-read staging and release the cache lock before touching a
potentially pageable caller buffer. Demand coalescing is capped at two pages in
0.75.6, the shape covered by the filesystem diagnostic; larger runs remain
disabled after intermittent failures in the long subsystem workload. This
second copy is intentional until a
caller-page pinning lifetime can cover the entire write. Copy bytes and both
lock-release classes are measured so a future change must demonstrate a real
gain without weakening fault safety.

SMP ownership
-------------

Kernel-heap metadata, virtual-range metadata, active page tables and physical
PMM/MemoryBlock state use distinct reentrant no-sleep owner locks. Their
mandatory order is heap, VM, page tables, then physical memory. Statistics
and invariants take the matching coherent owner boundary. If pressure would require a
reclaim operation while the VM boundary is held, allocation fails as
controlled OOM instead of yielding with an owner lock.

Unmap, decommit and address-space teardown publish a generation-bound TLB
shootdown to every other online CPU. A physical frame is freed only after all
targets acknowledged that generation. Missing acknowledgements are bounded:
the old PTE is restored, a recovery invalidation is attempted and the caller
receives a failure while the frame remains claimed. Contiguous extents use one
range request of at most 64 pages instead of one IPI round per page.

Pagefile I/O and VM eviction intentionally span filesystem/block waits and
therefore remain BSP-owned. A slot-backed page fault releases the no-sleep
metadata boundary before its existing busy/generation/lifecycle transaction;
the corresponding AP path fails closed. This is an explicit foundation limit,
not a claim that pageable VM is already parallel.

Safety invariants
-----------------

- Program payloads are not allocated or freed in IRQ context.
- Registry or heap mutations do not span yield, wait, I/O or asynchronous
  cancellation.
- A registry lock is never held while releasing external resources.
- Cleanup proceeds in reverse ownership order.
- `UnwindToken` protects the running task from kill during a sensitive
  operation; it is not a wait-spanning owner lock.
- A failed reclaim, page-in or inventory pass cannot be reported as an empty
  successful result.
- A block worker may borrow memory without a bounce copy only when the kernel
  caller proves the buffer resident, fixes its identity for the whole request,
  and remains the owner through late physical completion after a timeout.
- R4D DriverApi v19 can pin an existing resident driver buffer and map it into
  at most 64 owner-bound DMA segments. Inclusive address masks, power-of-two
  boundaries, per-segment alignment and length, and segment count are hard
  constraints. A permitted contiguous bounce mapping is explicit and keeps
  direction-correct copy ownership until unmap.
- Pin, mapping and legacy contiguous DMA handles are owner-checked. Mapping
  teardown precedes pin teardown; stale or duplicate release is rejected, and
  failed map paths never publish a partial segment list.
- The physical interval/ID/free-slot indices, the range address/ID indices,
  every range-local span list and their free lists must describe exactly the
  same active metadata. Boot self-tests validate those invariants after memory
  initialization and after heap activity.

The public VM, async-I/O and performance views are exposed through R4SYS and
R4DEV and described by the generated API reference.

R4D pinning is a bounded lifetime lease, not a pageable-memory facility. The
trusted driver must keep the virtual mapping and backing allocation intact
until all child mappings are unmapped. Calls that can allocate, copy or tear
down mappings run in task context; IRQ handlers complete hardware work and may
defer synchronization through the owner-bound driver work queue.

Partial release and sparse reservations (0.78.31)
------------------------------------------------
Demand-VM release walks the range's ordered commit spans and its existing
partial-uncommit cursor. It skips wholly uncommitted reservation gaps and
bounds each physical run to its current committed span. The unchanged
acknowledged unmap transaction still releases frames only after TLB completion.
The focused four-page model uses four mapping queries for both a 128-MB and
a 4-GB reservation; one failed extent adds one query on exact retry. These
are counted operations, not measured guest latency or throughput.

Before a heap tail is decommitted, its whole requested interval is removed
from the free bins and usable heap bound. The lower boundary tags remain
mapped. A partial failure keeps the exact offset/length in heap control and
blocks extension until that request succeeds. Existing lower free blocks
remain usable. pending_uncommit_bytes describes the withheld virtual tail;
it is not a count of still-resident frames. The normal heap capacity and
committed-byte view describe its usable prefix; VM/physical statistics retain
ownership of any unfinished suffix. Successful completion accounts the retired
pages exactly once.

The SDK applies the same rule to small-region tails: shrink the usable bound
and footer before the call, retain the request outside its memory, and resume
before growth. A direct cache entry becomes retiring before release; a failed
release can be retried but can never become an allocation hit. Reclaimed-byte
counts increase only after a complete confirmed request. The existing VM
owner registry remains the final authority for residual process-exit cleanup.

Pager identity and publication (0.78.32)
--------------------------------------
A slot reservation carries its own nonzero lifetime generation. Independent
reserve, release, error and I/O operations leave that identity unchanged.
Recovery/reset assigns a new incarnation; table-only inventory retains the
global change counter. Multiple initialized pages within one reservation also
keep their bindings when another page is written. Because validity is stored
per reservation, its first page-out must initialize the entire reservation.

Backing selection includes the path, cluster geometry and file size. Merely
probing a file does not change the active pager path. Selecting another backing
invalidates the former table; a stale I/O completion is rejected and cannot
reactivate it. Pager operations keep an owned path across filesystem waits.
The backend receives a VM-verified commit-coverage result for the exact offset
and page count. committed_bytes is an accounting sum, never an address limit.
Sparse high spans remain usable after lower committed spans are removed.

Demand-zero and page-in initialize a new frame through the permanent RAM
direct map before publishing its guest PTE. A second fault on the same busy
page waits on the existing scheduler WaitQueue mechanism and rechecks the
region ID and mapping after wake. Read failure never publishes partial bytes.
The VM no-sleep owner covers admission and final publication; filesystem I/O
and waits run outside it. A task unwind token protects each transaction, and
the region keeps a pager-I/O count across those waits. Uncommit/release returns
Busy internally (the existing VM_ERROR_MAP_FAILED ABI result) until it reaches
zero; the existing reaper can retry. Diagnostic page-state mutations are also
rejected while the region has a live pager transaction.

Page-out establishes an acknowledged hardware dirty epoch under the VM owner.
The original mappings stay alive during the write. At completion, hardware
and recorded dirty state are checked again under that owner. A concurrent
writer keeps the whole transfer resident and dirty, with its slot binding;
only a later quiet complete write can evict it. Thus a mixed or old on-disk
snapshot is never used as the authoritative image of a changed resident page.
The automatic pager and VM-region diagnostic page-out use the same transaction.
Failed writeback keeps resident ownership and dirty data. Pageable execution
and final writeback publication retain the existing BSP ownership contract;
DMA buffers must still follow their resident/pinned driver contract.

Seven focused host cases cover reservation reuse, backing replacement, sparse
coverage, controlled writes during I/O, incomplete reads, dirty-epoch failure
and a second waiting fault. These use the actual backing module and actual VM
function bodies with controlled scheduler/mapping/I/O edges. They are neither
hardware failure reproductions nor memory-pressure throughput measurements.

Resident owner stack budget (0.79.5)
-----------------------------------
R4X currently calls kernel APIs on its guarded, demand-grown program stack.
Before the first no-sleep or scheduler-runtime owner is acquired, the kernel
probes 64 KB below the current RSP, one page at a time. Any growth therefore
finishes on the resident fault IST stack before metadata mutation begins.
Nested owners perform no probes. Boot, resident Task and IST stacks are
outside the program-stack window and never receive these probes. Reads
preserve canaries; page-by-page traversal cannot skip a terminal guard.

Fault resolution is rejected if a no-sleep/runtime owner was already held
at exception entry. Reentrancy of an owner lock never authorizes recursive
mutation from a fault. A critical operation must fit the resident call budget
and prepare pageable caller data before taking its owner. The prefix probe
is a bounded cost at outer admission, not an eager commit of every reserved
program-stack page and not a second kernel-stack switch per public API call.

The graphics-buffer acceptance exposed a real fault inside physical AVL
removal. Growing that stack recursively mutated the same tree and the outer
call wrote a self-link on return. The isolated tree algorithm passed 20,000
bounded claims/releases; the fix changes owner admission, not AVL semantics.
The existing ProgramStorage selftest additionally enters at the moving stack
guard for both physical and runtime ownership, calls a 32-KB stack frame,
requires growth before admission and no growth while held, and checks the
real physical index. Its marker is STACKOWNER; no new recurring gate is added.

Graphics queue reservations (0.79.6)
-----------------------------------
Queue-read/write leases reserve the original BO backing before asynchronous
admission and transfer ownership to the kernel queue. They survive producer
reference release and process death. Conflicting cross-queue access requires
an explicit fence dependency. Logical cancel/timeout/reset does not release
physical device ownership; only proven quiescence allows the worker to retire
leases and collect VM/TLB backing. Wait publication has its own retained
reference, so a released slot cannot be reused beneath an Event waiter.

Array-value iteration of a mutable pool can place its entire contents on the
stack. The graphics worker hit its 64-KB guard with nested 17-KB job-table
copies. Queue and BO pool scans now iterate by address under their metadata
owner; they keep only individual entry snapshots. No worker stack increase
or fault-resolution exception is used. Details: Drivers/GrafikQueues07906.txt.

Resident R4D CPU allocations (0.79.10)
-------------------------------------
DriverApi30 heap_query exposes the existing kernel heap to authenticated R4D
init/shutdown and Driver Work contexts. It provides resident WB CPU virtual
addresses, at least 16-byte alignment and exact u64 payload lengths. It is not
a program VM allocator, DMA allocator or GPU virtual address space. A driver
must retain CPU backing through any separately admitted hardware borrow.

kernel/driver_heap.zig owns the runtime bridge; driver_memory_owner.zig owns
its metadata. Each heap allocation contains one intrusive treap record and
its aligned payload. There is no separate fixed allocation pool. Lookup has
expected logarithmic cost, independent of the fixed number of driver slots.
Only metadata and unwind enrollment run under the narrow scheduler runtime
owner. Heap operations and payload access occur after releasing that owner.

Every record binds to an exact non-reused driver-start epoch; allocation IDs
never wrap. Allocation publication racing close is retained privately. A free
detaches a record but keeps all accounting until the actual heap result; failure
reinserts the exact identity for retry. Successful free never dereferences its
old record again. Unwind enrollment covers the gaps between these phases.

DriverShutdown closes new allocation admission. Remaining CPU records are
reclaimed only after callbacks, IRQ, work and DMA cleanup have succeeded. A
pending operation or rejected heap free retains the closed owner and module.
Successful logical frees can leave kernel-owned heap VM tail capacity retained
internally; that capacity no longer belongs to the R4D. Existing loader tests
cover lifecycle races, failed release, handle exhaustion and 4096 real records;
the explicit NVIDIA runtime-check exercises the actual service in SMP4.

Dedicated R4D Task backing (0.79.10)
-----------------------------------
Driver threads use stable resident heap records with an intrusive identity
index, not a fixed Task pool. Record allocation, full guarded-stack/FPU Task
construction, exact Task retirement and record free happen outside short
runtime metadata sections. Initial unwind ownership predates Task registry
publication, so unpublished construction cannot be killed under its creator.
Failed construction/close races retain private records until cleanup succeeds.

Completed records can outlive their returned callbacks. Retained join leases
block removal; exact Task retirement precedes record free. Successful free
updates only copied accounting, while failure reinserts the same live backing.
Generic thread cleanup runs after shared work and before DMA/CPU allocations.
The service exposes no DMA mapping or GPU-address promise. Three host owner
cases cover lifecycle races and 4096 actual records; the explicit SMP4 NVIDIA
probe exercises real task stacks, resident buffers, waits and owner cleanup.

Resident R4D semaphore backing (0.79.10)
---------------------------------------
DriverApi33 semaphore records are real resident heap allocations indexed by
nonwrapping handles and driver-start epoch. Runtime critical sections cover
only metadata and queue enrollment, never heap allocation/free or parking.
An acquire lease prevents removal while a waiter is enrolled or returning.
Unpublished close-race records remain privately accounted; failed heap free
reinserts the same identity. Successful completion uses copied accounting and
never reads the freed record. The existing scheduler permit-handoff guard is
dropped under the runtime owner. After genuine callback/IRQ/work/task
quiescence, semaphore cleanup precedes DMA/CPU heap. Three existing-loader
host cases include real destruction, close races and 4096 allocated records;
the explicit SMP4 NVIDIA diagnostic exercises actual waits and cleanup.
